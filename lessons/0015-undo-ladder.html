The undo ladder: restore, reset, revert

You will be able to: pick the correct undo from where the change is (working tree, index, local commit, pushed commit).

"Undo" is not one command. Choose by where the change currently is:

The change is...Undo withResultSafe on shared history?
Edited, not stagedgit restore fileEdits gone for goodn/a (local)
Staged (git add)git restore --staged fileBack to unstaged; edits keptn/a
Committed, NOT pushed, want to keep editsgit reset --soft HEAD~1Commit gone; changes stagedn/a (unpushed)
Committed, not pushed, unstage toogit reset HEAD~1 (mixed)Commit gone; changes unstagedn/a
Committed, not pushed, throw awaygit reset --hard HEAD~1Commit and edits gone (reflog may help)n/a
Pushed / on shared branchgit revert <sha>New commit that undoes the oldYes

What reset moves

reset flagBranch movesIndex resetWorking files reset
--softyesnono
--mixed (default)yesyesno
--hardyesyesyes (data loss)
The ladder's top rung: if it was pushed and anyone may have pulled it, use revert, not reset.
