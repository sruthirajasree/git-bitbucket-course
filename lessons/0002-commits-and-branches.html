Commits and branches are just pointers

You will be able to: draw a commit graph, and explain what moves when you commit, branch, switch or reset.

A commit is a snapshot plus a pointer to its parent(s). A branch is just a movable label on one commit. HEAD says which branch you are on. That is the entire model.

Creating a branch costs nothing

git switch -c feature/PROJ-1 makes a second label on the same commit and moves HEAD to it.

Committing moves only the current branch

Why this matters: "undo" commands (reset, rebase, cherry-pick) are all moving labels or copying commits. Old commits remain until garbage collected, which is why the reflog can rescue you (lesson 17).
